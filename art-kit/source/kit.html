New theme art kit

Fruit Picnic is a cozy, cute merge-puzzle game (like 2048). Two matching tiles slide together and become the next one, so the art tells a little story that grows from tiny to grand. Each theme is a complete makeover of the game, and this kit has everything needed to create a new one.

Your style, your ideas. The examples in this kit show what the game looks like today, not rules. Feel free to bring your own style, shapes, colours and details. The only real limits are a few technical ones on page 4.

The story

Plan 14 tiles that grow from small and simple to big and special, so players feel a little reward each time a new one appears:

  • Small (2–16): these show up constantly, so they should be quick to recognise.
  • Middle (32–256) and Big (512–1024): rarer, more exciting things.
  • Finale (2048): the goal of the game, the most special tile. It also stands for the whole theme in the Settings theme picker.
  • Bonus (4096–16384): a secret “after the win” round for great players.

What to deliver: the whole theme

  • 14 tiles (2 to 16384)
  • Game screen: background, title style, colours (page 6)
  • Buttons: New game button, plus 2 round buttons with icons: leaderboard and settings
  • Score boxes and board look
  • Settings card: card style, sound and vibration icons, switch colour, theme picker look (page 7) NEW
  • Win screen: card style, win art, headline, confetti colours (page 8)
  • Lose screen: card style, lose art, headline (page 8)
  • Names: theme name and a name for each tile

Files

  • Tiles and art: SVG (vector) preferred, or PNG at 1024 × 1024 px
  • Win and lose art: transparent background
  • Icons: SVG, simple enough to read at about 22 px
  • Screens: a mockup of each screen (image or Figma) with the colours listed
  • Name tiles by value: tile-0002 … tile-16384

The technical bits

These are the only must-haves, so the art works in the game:

  • Tiles are square and fill the whole square, including their own background.
  • The game rounds the corners a little, so keep important details away from the very corners.
  • The top-left corner shows the tile’s number in a small white badge. Keep anything important out of that spot.
  • Tiles are small on phones (about 70–110 px). Each one should still be recognisable at that size.
  • Every tile must look different from the others at a glance, so players can tell them apart quickly.
  • Text must stay readable: title, scores and card text need enough contrast with what’s behind them.
  • Icons on the buttons and in Settings are shown at about 22 px, so keep them simple.
  • The 2048 tile doubles as the theme’s picture in Settings, at about 54 px.
  • Win and lose art is shown at about 150 × 150 px.

A full-size version of this guide is included as tile-template.png (for Procreate) and tile-template.svg (for Illustrator or Figma). The dashed area is just a helpful suggestion for where to keep key details.
